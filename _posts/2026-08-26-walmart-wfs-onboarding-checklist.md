# The Walmart WFS Onboarding Checklist: 5 Steps That Decide Whether You Get Approved

Walmart Fulfillment Services (WFS) is the closest analog to Amazon FBA in the US marketplace — and the harder one to get into. Walmart approves sellers for WFS selectively. The approval bar moves without warning, the rejection reasons are vague, and the application that worked six months ago may not work today.

Most sellers who get rejected do not understand why. They are told their account does not meet unspecified criteria. They improve what they think the criteria might be and reapply, and get rejected again. The criteria were never going to be satisfied because the real blocker was a missing step they did not know to take.

This article walks through the five steps that determine WFS approval, the common rejection reasons at each, and the seller profile that tends to get accepted on the first try.

## Why Walmart Approval Matters

WFS is a structural advantage on Walmart Marketplace. Two-day shipping, the "Fulfilled by Walmart" badge, and inclusion in Walmart's fulfillment-filtered search are not free. They are earned by sellers who clear a vetting process that the marketplace does not fully document.

The opportunity is real. Walmart Marketplace is the second-largest US ecommerce platform, and WFS listings outrank non-WFS listings in most categories. For sellers currently running FBM (Fulfilled by Merchant), converting to WFS typically lifts conversion rates between 15 and 35 percent on the same ASIN at the same price.

The cost of getting rejected is not just the application fee. Every month spent outside WFS is a month of lost conversion lift, lost badge eligibility, and lost positioning. For a seller making a serious commitment to Walmart, getting approved quickly is itself a competitive advantage.

## Step 1: Account Standing on Walmart Marketplace

Walmart does not approve WFS applications from sellers whose existing Marketplace account has unresolved issues. The application form does not list "account standing" as a checklist item. It is enforced at the review stage.

What gets checked:

- Order defect rate below 2 percent over the last 90 days
- Cancellation rate below 2 percent over the last 90 days
- Late shipment rate below 4 percent over the last 90 days
- No active policy violations or seller performance cases
- Customer service response rate above 90 percent on open cases

A seller who is two months into a Walmart Marketplace account but has not yet hit these thresholds will be rejected. There is no override. The standard answer from Walmart support is to maintain good performance and reapply after the 90-day window has accumulated enough clean data.

Practical recommendation: do not apply for WFS until you have at least 90 days of Marketplace history with metrics inside the thresholds. Sellers who rush this step waste an application cycle and add 90 days to their WFS timeline.

---

## Step 2: SKU Eligibility

Not every product category is eligible for WFS. Walmart restricts the program in three ways:

- **Category restrictions.** Certain categories are not approved for WFS fulfillment at all. The list includes hazardous materials, perishable food, live plants, and oversized items. Walmart does not maintain a single published list — eligibility is checked at the SKU level.

- **Size and weight limits.** WFS has hard limits on inbound shipment size and weight. Items exceeding 150 pounds or 108 inches in length are rejected. Bulky but non-oversized items (50-150 pounds) require special freight handling that increases cost.

- **Restricted products.** Items requiring age verification, items with regulated components, and items with Walmart-flagged IP concerns are filtered out. The filter is not transparent.

Before applying, a seller should pre-screen every intended SKU against current WFS eligibility. The fastest way to do this is to upload the SKU list to Walmart's WFS eligibility tool inside Seller Center — the tool reports per-SKU eligibility in seconds.

Common mistake: applying for WFS approval at the account level, then discovering 40 percent of the seller's catalog is ineligible. The approval is granted; the inventory that can be sent is far smaller than expected.

---

## Step 3: Inventory Preparation Capability

Walmart expects WFS sellers to maintain stable inventory levels. Approval is partly a judgment about whether the seller can sustain 14+ days of in-stock coverage on the SKUs they send to WFS.

What gets evaluated:

- Sales velocity on existing Marketplace listings
- Inventory history on Marketplace (FBM)
- Supplier reliability for restock
- Storage capacity at the seller's origin

A seller with strong Marketplace sales but no documented restock pipeline will be flagged. Walmart does not want WFS inventory to sit at 60-day cover because the seller cannot refill. The economic model depends on steady turn.

For new sellers without existing Marketplace history, the answer is to demonstrate restock capability through documentation — supplier agreements, inventory photos, POs for upcoming shipments. Walmart's WFS team has discretion here.

---

## Step 4: Operational Standards

Walmart's WFS standard requires sellers to comply with preparation, labeling, and shipping requirements that are stricter than FBM norms.

Key requirements:

- **Labeling compliance.** Every unit must have a scannable barcode (UPC, EAN, or GTIN-14) that matches the listing. Re-labeling is not allowed — if the existing barcode is non-compliant, the unit is unsellable.

- **Poly-bagging rules.** Certain product categories require poly-bagging with suffocation warnings. The rules differ by category and Walmart updates them more frequently than Amazon's. The seller is responsible for tracking the current standard.

- **Box content rules.** Multi-pack SKUs must have the correct inner pack count. Mixed-SKU boxes are prohibited. Walmart's receiving scanners flag mismatches and the entire carton can be refused.

- **Inbound shipping requirements.** Walmart requires approved carrier routing for inbound shipments. Self-arranged freight is rejected at the warehouse. The seller books through Walmart's partnered carriers or uses Walmart's negotiated rates.

Operational standards are not enforced by approval committee judgment — they are enforced at the warehouse door. The first inbound shipment teaches the seller where their prep was non-compliant. The lesson costs time and freight.

Practical recommendation: before sending the first full WFS shipment, send a small test shipment of 20-50 units. Verify receipt, check-in, and listing status. Scale only after the test clears.

---

## Step 5: Pricing and Promotional Discipline

Walmart's marketplace is built on price competitiveness. WFS sellers are expected to maintain parity with — or undercut — competing sellers on the same ASIN. Approval is harder for sellers whose current Marketplace pricing is significantly above the category median.

What gets evaluated:

- Current pricing relative to category average for top SKUs
- Promotional participation (Rollbacks, Clearance, seasonal events)
- Restraint on price gouging during high-demand periods

Walmart does not publish the exact price-to-median ratio that triggers rejection. The observed pattern is sellers priced more than 15-20 percent above the category median on their main SKUs get flagged. Sellers at parity or below pass cleanly.

For sellers preparing a WFS application, aligning pricing to category median before applying is a low-cost step that meaningfully improves approval odds. Running a 7-day 10 percent promotional leading into the application signals competitive intent.

---

## Common Rejection Reasons

When sellers are rejected, the rejection email is typically terse. The seller performance team lists one or two stated reasons, often generic. The actual block can usually be inferred from which step the seller is weakest on:

- "Performance metrics do not meet program requirements" — Step 1 (account standing)
- "Catalog not eligible for WFS" — Step 2 (SKU eligibility)
- "Operational readiness not demonstrated" — Step 3 or 4 (inventory or prep)
- "Pricing not aligned with marketplace expectations" — Step 5 (pricing discipline)

Most rejections map to Step 1 or Step 5. The two are easy to miss because neither is on the application form. The form collects company info, tax info, and banking. The evaluation happens against data Walmart already has on the seller.

## What It Does Well

- **Time.** A pre-application audit that takes 2-3 hours across Seller Center, spreadsheets, and category searches takes a single prompt. Re-running the audit after fixing issues is one prompt plus the fix.

- **Reproducible.** The same five-step audit runs for every seller. Once written, the prompt is a template that can be applied across accounts or marketplaces.

- **Open source.** The MCP server is open source on GitHub. Prompts are plain English. No proprietary query language.

- **Cross-tool.** The audit pulls marketplace performance, SKU eligibility, pricing comparison, inventory history, and promotional data in one pass. The cross-step view — which blockers stack — is where the audit value lives.

---

## Where It Falls Short

It does not bypass Walmart's review discretion. Even a seller who passes all five audit steps can be rejected for reasons outside the data Walmart publishes. The audit reduces rejection probability; it does not eliminate it.

It does not validate operational readiness physically. The audit reports on data Walmart can see. Whether the seller's warehouse can pack, label, and palletize to WFS standard is verified at inbound, not in advance.

It depends on having existing Walmart Marketplace data. Sellers brand new to Walmart cannot run the audit — they have no 90-day history to evaluate. The audit is for sellers who already sell on Walmart and want to add WFS, not for first-time Walmart sellers.
